The Recommendation Letter Memo

How to Select the Right Witnesses for Your Application

Recommendation letters are third-party evidence. Their authority comes from proximity: who has observed the applicant closely, which claims that person can verify, and what evidence they can carry into the file with credibility.

This edition develops a method for selecting recommenders, distributing evidence across letters, identifying the claims that require witness, and giving each recommendation a distinct role inside the larger admissions argument.

Inside the Edition
  • Witness selection and proximity
  • Prestige, credibility, and direct observation
  • Macro narrative and Micro proof
  • Evidence distribution across recommenders
  • Recommendation timing and coordination
  • Repairing gaps in third-party evidence

The Carry Sentence

How Admissions Committees Remember, Repeat, and Defend a File

Selective admissions depends partly on compression. A complex application eventually becomes something another reader can remember, repeat, and defend.

The Carry Sentence develops the logic behind that compression: how an application earns a governing sentence, how the sentence accumulates evidence across the file, and how memorable positioning survives the movement from individual reading into committee.

Inside the Edition
  • The file as a carried argument
  • Memory, compression, and reader language
  • The elements of a durable carry sentence
  • Coordination across essays and activities
  • Recommendation support and external proof
  • Making the governing sentence survive committee

The Activities Ledger

How Extracurricular Work Becomes Proof in Elite Admissions

The activity list is a record of what the applicant chose to build, lead, produce, change, study, or sustain outside the classroom.

The Activities Ledger develops a method for converting that work into readable evidence through role, artifact, metric, progression, hierarchy, and consequence. The result is an extracurricular record with clearer signal and stronger proof.

Inside the Edition
  • Activity as evidence
  • Role, artifact, and consequence
  • Metrics and proof density
  • Hierarchy across the activities list
  • Progression and sustained commitment
  • Coordination with the larger application
